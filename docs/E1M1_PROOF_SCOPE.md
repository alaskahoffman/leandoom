## Recursive sound execution and the firing return

`./scripts/verify-original-noise-recursive` reuses the pinned original byte
regions and introduces no decoder or runtime-engine changes. The new
`E1M1NoiseRecursive.eval` is a recursive summary of the original machine in two
phases: sector entry and line traversal. Sector entry either executes the return
epilogue or initializes the sector's line loop. Each line dispatches through the
proved opening/neighbor/block checks, recursively evaluates a called child,
checks its actual return address, advances the parent, and either processes the
remaining lines or executes the parent's real RET. Children may themselves have
nonempty line lists; there is no terminal-child premise or arbitrary callee
transition assumption.

`execution` proves by induction that every successful evaluation is a prefix of
the original decoded instruction program. It requires the correct phase entry
PC and the equality `eval fuel phase initial = some final`. The evaluator checks
opening returns, recursive return addresses and expected continuation PCs;
failed checks or insufficient fuel produce `none`. Success always includes the
current sector's actual return. This is a conditional execution theorem, not a
proof that every reachable sector graph succeeds for some particular budget.

Both the recursive child and the remaining parent lines receive the smaller
structural fuel. The conservative instruction bound therefore satisfies
`bound 0 = 0` and `bound (k+1) = 2*bound k + 71`. This tree-shaped upper bound is
not a runtime estimate, recursion-depth claim, or the live worklist's bound.
`zero_fuel` explicitly rejects completion at zero fuel, including a pruned call.

`alert_execution` adds the original sound-target/counter/subsector entry before
recursive propagation (bound `8+bound k`). `fire_execution` joins the existing

## Constructed recursive noise traversal and conditional termination

`./scripts/verify-original-noise-termination` adds `E1M1NoiseTermination`.
This removes the supplied-finite-trace premise from the new completion theorem.
Earlier sections describe intermediate conditional results; their outstanding
trace-construction obligation is discharged here under the following entry
conditions.

`World` groups static allocation, graph-word storage, bounded line-array sizes,
coverage of every sector's lines, and closure of every two-sided edge within the
sector table. `Ready` groups the initial ledger and its address layout, actual
memory representations of the map and graph, the current sector or live line
cursor, and a legal incoming sound-block count. A stack reserve is also required.
These certificates describe the original machine's memory. Their establishment
from the fixed DOS E1M1 startup is still separate work.

The construction uses the natural-number measure

```text
credit(ledger) * (maxLines + 1) + pendingLines + 1
```

Here `pendingLines` is zero at sector entry and the parent's remaining-line
count during scanning. The proof covers all six recursive trace constructors:

- A pruned or empty sector finishes directly. An admitted nonempty sector
  spends at least one ledger credit before starting at most `maxLines` lines.
- A skipped line advances the original cursor, decreasing pending lines.
- A recursive call enters a sector with zero pending lines. Even an immediately
  pruned child therefore decreases the measure from its active parent's scan.
- After constructing the child's trace, the existing accounting and frame
  proofs derive its remaining credit, saved registers, return address, and
  memory preservation. Parent resumption decreases pending lines, while credit
  cannot increase.

`call_word` proves the actual recursive CALL wrote `0x27006` to its incoming
stack word. The child's restored-frame theorem then establishes the real return
address; it is no longer a caller-supplied fact. Child traces and their guards
are constructed by induction, not supplied as successful execution hypotheses.
The existing trace theorems are used only after constructing a smaller child.

`construct` returns a finite `CheckedTrace` from the entry certificates and
credit-sized stack space. `entry_reserve` bounds initial ledger credit directly
by `2*N`, without referring to any execution. Consequently `sector_total` needs
only `56*N+20` bytes below entry ESP, room for its incoming return word, and the
static/initial certificates. It proves that the original summary evaluator
returns with `2*N*(L+1)+1` fuel, restores the caller, and preserves the map and
graph relations. `sector_execution` adds the original entry PC and connects the
constructed evaluation to the existing pinned-instruction execution model.
The instruction-count bound still uses the conservative recursive `bound`
function; the evaluator-fuel bound is not a wall-clock timing claim.

This is **termination under explicit entry and storage invariants**, not
termination for arbitrary machine memory, a DOS startup theorem, live Lean
worklist correspondence, or whole-game equivalence. No game/runtime code changes.

addresses and separation of two- or four-byte protected reads. The copied

to four bytes.

## Original candidate acceptance and block splitting

`OriginalGroupSplit` extends the executable coverage to `0x396f8..0x39731`.
Its candidate theorem executes the owner and signed size checks: occupied and
undersized candidates return to the search loop at `0x396bd`; a suitable free
candidate reaches the split decision. It does not assert that the search loop
has found this candidate from the initial heap.

The split theorem executes wrapping subtraction of the requested block size,
the original signed comparison, and both outcomes. A remainder of exactly 64
bytes stays with the allocated block; splitting occurs only above 64. The split
path executes all eight original stores: free remainder owner/tag/size/previous
fields, its next link, the successor's previous link, and the selected block's
next link and size. The successor load is modeled after the first four writes,
retaining alias behavior rather than silently treating every load as an initial
memory read. `byte_partition` proves the modular size identity, while `frame`
protects words separated from the complete write footprint. These instruction
semantics alone do not establish a globally well-formed heap or payload capacity.

and pointer facts across the combined path.

## Original allocator entry and counting-to-allocation composition

`OriginalGroupMallocEntry` pins the actual allocator CALL at `0x2e639` and
entry bytes at `0x39690..0x396f8`. Its proofs execute the four register saves,
request rounding, rover load, predecessor-owner test, and either choice of
starting block. The low-byte `AND CL, 0xfc` is proved equivalent to masking the
full word with `0xfffffffc`, using kernel-checked bit reasoning. The complete
request computation is wrapping 32-bit `((n + 3) & 0xfffffffc) + 24`, including
overflow cases. For E1M1's 2,568-byte payload, this gives a 2,592-byte block.

The first traversal requires a free starting block and a non-exhausted search.
It executes the next-link load and reaches the existing candidate gate. These
conditions are facts about the explicit prefix summary, not assumptions that
the machine has already completed the desired execution. No general heap
search, coalescing, purging or allocation failure behavior is claimed here.

`OriginalGroupMalloc.execution` joins this path through candidate acceptance,
either split outcome, allocation stores, the actual RET, and grouping entry
through `M_ClearBox`, within 78 instructions from the CALL. The incoming user
and tag, suitable-first-free conditions and return-slot write separation remain
explicit. The saved return-address value is now derived from the CALL itself,
including preservation across the four register saves, without a caller-supplied
return-address premise. A separate frame contract protects counts and cached
sector pointers through all writes, including stack slots reused by the caller.

`OriginalGroupStartup.execution` composes the full counting pass with that
allocation theorem in one machine state, within `87 + 17 * lines.length`
instructions (8,162 for 475 lines). It derives user/tag from the counting pass.
Its remaining premises include the counting layout/input relation and the heap
conditions on the computed post-count state. `sector_count` and `cached_inputs`
transport the counting results through allocation under byte separation. The
name does not imply a proof of all game startup: heap construction, initial
counter initialization, and the enclosing grouping loop are still unproved.

`./scripts/verify-original-group-malloc` re-extracts all composed byte intervals,
checks their file mappings and relocation boundaries, and audits 85 declarations
under the three-axiom allowlist. Seven regression tests cover altered CALL,

incorrect results and unsuitable heaps. A concrete one-line kernel fixture

sector count, the derived return slot, and the rounded request. It makes this
conditional composition nonvacuous without asserting that its synthetic heap

The independent native diagnostics execute the actual CALL through allocation

## Raw heap facts before the counting pass

`OriginalGroupHeap` replaces the allocator's computed-state readiness premise
with a local header view and static conditions on the memory before counting.
`View` identifies the zone, rover, predecessor and its owner, chosen block's
previous/next links, free-owner field and size. Its eight word reads are checked
against the initial memory. This is deliberately a local view: it does not claim
the entire heap is well formed or that startup has constructed these headers.

`prefix_frame` accounts for all five writes from the actual CALL and four
register saves, including wrapping stacks. `saved_view` and `candidate_fields`
prove these writes preserve the separated header words and derive the actual
chosen block and search sentinel for either predecessor-owner branch.

The initial `Conditions` record requires a non-exhausted search, the original
signed size comparison, and byte separation. The successor-link separation
protects the load that occurs after the first four split stores. Separate
conditions protect the global zone pointer through splitting and the owner
store, and the saved return through split and allocation writes. `ready` derives
the previous `OriginalGroupMalloc.Ready` contract from these facts. In particular,
no predicate on an already computed split state is an input to this theorem.
The split footprint is conservatively protected even in unsplit cases, so these
conditions are sufficient, not claimed to be necessary for every valid heap.

`count_view` transports the raw header view through every counter increment.
`count_ready` combines it with the counting pass's derived request and unchanged
stack pointer. `execution` then runs counting through allocation and grouping
entry under initial-memory premises, with the existing `87 + 17 * lines.length`
bound. This removes the post-count allocator premise from the public composition;
it does not remove the need to prove initial header values and separation from
earlier startup operations.

`./scripts/verify-original-group-heap` re-extracts the same pinned byte intervals

remainders at 64 and 65 bytes, and ordinary/wrapping stacks. These are synthetic

The next heap obligation is to establish these raw facts from the preceding

## A preceding allocation establishes the free-block view

The pinned startup operation diagnostic identifies the allocation immediately

pointer. Replaying the 1,272 preceding operations produces a free rover at

This replay is evidence for choosing the next proof, not a proof of original
startup control flow or a source of data for the live engine.

`OriginalGroupOwned` certifies the original nonzero-owner metadata branch from
`0x39731` to `0x39771`, using the previously pinned allocation interval. The

The later zone and next-link loads occur after both owner stores, preserving
alias behavior. Its frame theorem protects words separated from all five stores.
This theorem stops before register restoration and RET.

`HeapWrites` supplies reusable frame and assigned-value theorems for ordered

free-remainder view theorem requires the splitting case.

## What remains to connect

The inactive DOS automap flag is still an explicit initial memory relation.
We have proved the Lean adapter defaults and preservation by this lethal-floor
segment, **not** a DOS startup-and-all-tics invariant. The input restriction alone
is not presented as that missing theorem.

Player preparation now connects through lethal floor damage and the weapon
selection and use gate into the psprite prologue and first-slot decision.
The loop continuation, flash-coordinate synchronization and return are now
proved for the no-flash-transition path, with explicit seam conditions. The
general setter control flow and slot-to-setter entry are now proved as well.
Action-free state chains now compose through their actual returns and the flash
continuation. The three flash-light actions, supported flash expiration paths,
and production flash-update correspondence are proved too. Raising and ready
paths now include the player-pose reset and actual nested returns, under the
contracts described above. Remaining steps include reachable-state and
caller-stack invariants, remaining weapon actions and full `Weapon.tick`
correspondence. Fresh use
also requires the `P_UseLines` callee and its geometry/action dispatch. No-damage and nonlethal
branches still need to be joined into this same enclosing theorem. Later
animation actions, including scream/fall, and complete death handling across
future tics are still beyond this proof. The initial state-entry countdowns
above are distinguished from the adjusted countdowns now proved after the draw.
The broader automap shutdown proofs remain available, but extending automap
keyboard/cheat dispatch is not on the immediate fixed-profile path.

After that, continue composing the reachable player/world update paths, establish
the initial state and invariant preservation, and connect rendering and final
observable events to the interaction correspondence theorem. Other causes of
damage and all reachable E1M1 interactions still need coverage. This new theorem
is one conditional segment, not universal E1M1 or pixel-for-pixel equivalence.
